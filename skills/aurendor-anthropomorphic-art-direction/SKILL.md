# AURENDOR Anthropomorphic Art Direction

## Exact job

Make invisible operations understandable by giving systems, objects, or materials controlled human-like behavior. The default is expressive behavior without a face. Anthropomorphism must clarify the message and preserve enterprise credibility.

## Levels

- **0 — None:** the concept is clearer without human behavior.
- **1 — Material behavior:** alignment, tension, attraction, avoidance, protection, handoff, or recovery is expressed through motion/material only.
- **2 — Object behavior:** objects imply posture, attention, teamwork, friction, or relief without a literal face.
- **3 — System entity:** a controlled recurring entity has a silhouette, gesture language, and interaction rules; facial features remain optional.
- **4 — Character:** face/limbs and explicit personality require a consistency sheet, recurrence plan, and owner-approved role.
- **5 — Mascot universe:** a named long-term mascot/cast requires separate brand strategy and owner approval; never introduce it within an ordinary post.

Prefer levels 0–3. Use 4 only when the face test and recurrence need justify it. Level 5 is out of scope without explicit approval.

## Decision tests

1. What abstract behavior is being made visible?
2. Does anthropomorphism reduce explanation or merely add charm?
3. If the face is removed, does the idea still work? If yes, remove it.
4. Is the emotional register composed, intelligent, and restrained rather than cute, frantic, or toy-like?
5. Does the behavior stay within the product’s real capabilities, avoiding claims of consciousness, judgment, empathy, or autonomous authority?
6. Can the silhouette, materials, scale, joints, gesture, and lighting remain consistent across the required frames?
7. Does it coexist with Arabic typography without turning Arabic into a caption around a mascot?

## Direction packet

Specify level, humanized behavior, comprehension benefit, face requirement/result, emotional register, capability boundary, childishness risk, silhouette, materials, motion/gesture grammar, scale, interaction rules, forbidden expressions, and consistency anchors. For recurring level 3–4 entities, require front/side/three-quarter views and a gesture/material sheet before campaign production.

## Hard boundaries

- No generic smiling robot, floating assistant orb, stock eyes-on-object trick, kawaii face, or costume mascot by default.
- No face added only to increase engagement.
- No implied sentience, emotion reading, human replacement, or unverified autonomous action.
- No copying a reference character’s silhouette, face, proportions, palette behavior, or world.
- No raw reference pixels or named-source imitation in generation.

## Quality gate

Pass only when anthropomorphism improves textless comprehension, the face test is recorded, capability boundaries are truthful, Arabic/typography remains primary where applicable, and the result feels premium at mobile scale.

## Failure behavior

Reduce to the lowest level that preserves meaning. If consistency cannot be maintained, replace the character with object/material behavior. `CHILDISH_ANTHROPOMORPHISM`, `INCONSISTENT_CHARACTER`, or generic-robot failure requires concept restart.

## Eval cases

1. **Dashboard tiles cooperate through alignment:** Level 1–2 can pass without faces.
2. **Smiling robot explains AI:** Fail as generic and unnecessarily literal.
3. **One-off face whose removal changes nothing:** Remove the face.
4. **Recurring level-4 character:** Block without owner approval and a consistency system.
