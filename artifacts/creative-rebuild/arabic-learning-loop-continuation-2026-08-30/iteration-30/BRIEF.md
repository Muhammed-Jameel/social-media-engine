# Iteration 30 — Start With a Hypothesis

## Objective

Turn controlled comparison into one practical experimentation rule: start with an evidence-based hypothesis, randomly compare an original and a variant, and define the success measure before reading the result.

## Evidence scope

HMRC describes controlled digital experiments as random allocation between an original control and a redesigned variant, anchored by an evidence-based hypothesis and a primary success measure. Separate GOV.UK guidance likewise specifies a control, variation, random split, planned duration/sample, and outcome comparison. The post compresses that structure; it does not claim that two versions always produce a definitive answer or that every change is safe to experiment on.

## Experiment boundary

Teams need an adequate sample and duration, correct randomization, statistically responsible analysis, and guardrail measures for unintended harm. Accessibility, privacy, consent expectations, operational risk, and fairness still apply. An inconclusive or worse result is learning, not permission to cherry-pick another metric.
