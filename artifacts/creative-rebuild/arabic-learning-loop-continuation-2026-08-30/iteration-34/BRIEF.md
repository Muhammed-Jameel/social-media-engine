# Iteration 34 — The Market Extends Beyond Storefronts

## Objective

Turn city-level evidence about informal business premises into a market-research prompt: storefronts are only one place to look for businesses and customers.

## Exact copy

- Metadata: `بحث سوق · ٤٦`
- Headline: `لا تبحث عن السوق` / `في الواجهات فقط`
- Premises layers: `واجهة` / `منزل` / `مكان غير ثابت`
- Cities: `بغداد · البصرة · النجف · السليمانية`
- Support: `في مسح ٢٠٢١ لأربع مدن عراقية، تراوحت الأعمال غير المسجّلة العاملة من المنازل بين ١٣٪ و٣٥.٢٪، ومن أماكن غير ثابتة بين ٢.٤٪ و٣٠.٧٪ بحسب المدينة.`
- Source: `المصدر: مسح منشآت القطاع غير الرسمي، مجموعة البنك الدولي، ٢٠٢١.`

## Evidence scope

The World Bank Group's 2021 Informal Sector Enterprise Surveys profile reports city-specific estimates for unregistered businesses in Baghdad, Basrah, Najaf, and Sulaymaniyah. The share operating from within a household ranged from 13.0% to 35.2%; the share operating from non-fixed premises, including hawkers, ranged from 2.4% to 30.7%. Each city sample is representative of informal businesses in that city, not of Iraq as a whole.

The market-research headline is an inference: sampling only visible storefronts can miss parts of the business population described by the survey. It is not a source conclusion about how every study must recruit participants.

## Concept tournament

1. **Selected — layered premises directory:** one solid storefront block is only the back layer; tactile home and non-fixed-place tabs extend beyond it.
2. Rejected — Iraq map with city markers: would imply geographic precision the profile does not provide and repeat data-map behavior.
3. Rejected — shop, house, and street-vendor illustration: risks visual stereotype and treats very different businesses as one scene.
4. Rejected — giant range pairs: would turn a research implication into a data card and burden the viewer with four hero numbers.
5. Rejected — storefront photograph: would privilege the exact visible-premises bias the post asks researchers to question.

## Composition constraints

- 1080×1350, Arabic-only visible copy, RTL, 68 px text safe margin.
- Pale full-canvas editorial with one dark storefront block, one bright home tab, and one bordered non-fixed-place tab.
- Layer depth comes from native CSS rotation, borders, and restrained shadows; no simulated UI.
- Four city names, both range pairs, unregistered-business scope, year, and source remain visible.
- Support and source remain readable at 324×405.
- No map, city marker, shop icon, house icon, hawker image, person, storefront photograph, dashboard, generated image, or external visual asset.

## Professional design principles studied

- A stable editorial framework can hold several voices or content layers without losing hierarchy.
- Physical paper strips can give typography dimension and make layers feel materially distinct.
- A secondary visual narrative can cross or protrude beyond the primary layout boundary when the main reading order remains controlled.

References were used for principle study only. No composition, strip, fold, grid, type treatment, color, texture, photograph, or project pixel was reused.

## Evidence boundary

Operating from a home or non-fixed premises is not synonymous with being informal, low-quality, temporary, or small. The profile studies unregistered businesses and does not establish why a business chose its premises, whether customers visit it, or which recruitment channel reaches it. The city ranges must not be averaged or described as a national percentage.
