# Iteration 24 — Design for Wider Access

## Objective

Expand the feed into digital accessibility with source-backed, non-paternalistic Arabic copy and an inclusive visual behavior that does not simulate disability or assistive technology.

## Exact copy

- Metadata: `وصول رقمي · ٣٦`
- Headline: `صمّم الخدمة الرقمية`
- Hero: `لأوسع نطاق` / `من الناس`
- Support: `اجعل المحتوى قابلًا للإدراك والتشغيل والفهم، ومتوافقًا مع أدوات المساعدة.`
- Source: `المصدر: إرشادات إتاحة محتوى الويب ٢.٢، اتحاد شبكة الويب العالمية.`

## Evidence scope

WCAG 2.2 covers recommendations for making web content accessible to a wider range of people with disabilities and organizes accessibility under perceivable, operable, understandable, and robust principles. The post paraphrases that purpose and the first three user-facing principles while expressing robustness as compatibility with assistive tools. It does not claim universal access, WCAG conformance, legal compliance, or a tested AURENDOR/client result.

## Concept tournament

1. **Selected — open-frame editorial:** one large frame with four access gaps surrounds the Arabic hero; no gap is privileged as the single route.
2. Rejected — simulated Braille: visually appropriative without verified Braille content and tactile production.
3. Rejected — person using assistive technology: high stereotype and generic-stock risk; unnecessary to communicate the principle.
4. Rejected — four-icon WCAG grid: checklist-like, visually generic, and too close to an interface explainer.

## Composition constraints

- 1080×1350, Arabic-only visible copy, RTL, 68 px safe margin.
- Dark AURENDOR field with one pale open frame and four neon access points.
- The frame is a composition device, not a compliance diagram or functional interface.
- Source and support must remain readable at 324×405.
- No disability stereotype, simulated assistive UI, Braille, icon set, checklist, legal badge, or external visual asset.

## Quality target

All critics ≥145/160; evidence fidelity, Arabic, source legibility, contrast, mobile, originality, inclusion framing, and feed gates required; production disabled.
