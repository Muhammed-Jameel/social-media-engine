# Iteration 29 — Measure Completion Rate

## Objective

Move from customer-language guidance to one exact service-performance metric: define completion rate from completed transactions and all transactions started.

## Evidence scope

The GOV.UK Service Manual defines a service's completion rate as completed digital transactions divided by all digital transactions started, including partially completed or failed transactions. It requires clearly defined transaction start and end points. The post teaches that definition; it does not present an AURENDOR result, target, benchmark, or invented percentage.

## Concept tournament

1. **Selected — typographic transaction ledger:** the numerator, division bar, denominator, and result become the page architecture; enlarged mathematical marks form a separate dark rail.
2. Rejected — funnel chart: a generic analytics metaphor that would suggest stage data not present in the source.
3. Rejected — progress bar: interface simulation and another linear completion device.
4. Rejected — scanned receipt: unnecessary external texture and false transaction details.

## Composition constraints

- 1080×1350, Arabic-only visible copy, RTL, 68 px right safe margin.

- Numerator, denominator, and fraction bar must remain readable at 324×405.
- No invented percentage, chart, dashboard, funnel, progress bar, fake receipt, interface, external asset, generated image, or professional reference pixel.

## Metric boundary

Completion rate applies to genuine transactions with defined starts and ends; general page visits, enquiries, or informal complaints are not automatically transactions. Completion does not mean an application was approved. Teams should exclude internal/test traffic and bots, account for supported and save-and-return journeys, segment where appropriate, and interpret completion alongside satisfaction, cost, take-up, and research.
