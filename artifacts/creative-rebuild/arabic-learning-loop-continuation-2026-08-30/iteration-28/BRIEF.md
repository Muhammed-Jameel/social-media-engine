# Iteration 28 — Name Things as People Do

## Objective

Extend the user-research sequence into customer language with one practical content-design behavior: learn the words people use for a service and write with familiar, clear language.

## Exact copy

- Metadata: `لغة الناس · ٤٠`
- Headline: `سمِّ الأشياء`
- Diagonal continuation: `كما يسمّيها الناس`
- Language trail: `يقولونها` / `يبحثون بها` / `يفهمونها`
- Support: `تعلّم كلماتهم من البحث، وراجع ما يبحثون عنه، ثم اكتب بعبارات مألوفة وواضحة.`
- Reference: `مرجع: دليل أبحاث المستخدم وإرشادات اللغة الواضحة، بوابة الحكومة البريطانية.`

## Evidence scope

The GOV.UK Service Manual says observing real users helps teams learn the language people use when talking about a service. GOV.UK's clear-language guidance recommends familiar words that users use themselves and suggests reviewing common search terms. W3C's supplemental cognitive-accessibility pattern recommends common, clear words and removing or explaining uncommon jargon. The post compresses those principles into a practical sequence; it does not claim that one quote, one search term, or one research session validates a label.

## Concept tournament

1. **Selected — diagonal public-language specimen:** a complete vernacular phrase occupies one live-type diagonal lane, with a three-beat speech/search/comprehension trail below it.
2. Rejected — quotation bubble: a generic social-media symbol that would turn research into a decorative testimonial.
3. Rejected — terminology card grid: another interface-like comparison system and a repeat of earlier modular structures.
4. Rejected — correction proof: too close to Iteration 27's observation-versus-assumption mechanism.

## Composition constraints

- 1080×1350, Arabic-only visible copy, RTL, 68 px safe margin.
- Deep AURENDOR green field, one pale diagonal lane, one continuous live Arabic phrase.
- Complete headline required; no intentional clipping of Arabic letters.
- Source remains at least 10.2 px at the archived 324×405 review size.
- No quotation bubble, sticky note, interface card, terminology grid, correction mark, photograph, external asset, generated image, or professional reference pixel.

## Content boundary

Different user groups may use different words. Search language is evidence, not a verdict; teams still need inclusive research, task context, testing, and analysis. Necessary legal or specialist terms may remain when they are explained clearly.

All critics ≥145/160; evidence, Arabic, mobile, no-crop, originality, complete-feed, and content-boundary gates required; production disabled.
