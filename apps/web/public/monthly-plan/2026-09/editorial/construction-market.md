# Iraq construction market → Bunyan Pro editorial decisions

Research cutoff and source inspection: **10 September 2026**. This is an editorial market study for September11–30, not a market-size forecast or an independent evaluation of Bunyan. The deliverable is ten complete Arabic briefs in `pillars/bunyan.json`: seven carousels and three 56-second scripts. Video production is deliberately deferred. Seven primary web sources were read and snapshotted; current product source was inspected read-only. All public teaching scenarios are explicitly fictional. No customer interviews, sales calls, outcome tests or statistical sampling were invented.

## What the evidence changes

The strongest implication is that “construction delays” is too broad a product promise. Payment arrears, procurement lead times, changes in regulation, formal approvals, drawing defects and coordination failures do not have the same owner or remedy. A product that records updates can help a contractor see and discuss a constraint. It cannot fund a customer, produce an official permit or make an unresolved design safe to execute. Our content therefore starts from a specific decision an owner can improve: which site needs attention, what changed in a requested variation, or whether a payment request has enough context to review.

The University of Baghdad’s 2023 study concerns large Iraqi hospital projects delivered through EPC/turnkey arrangements. Its abstract identifies late contractor payments, materials, official approvals, document errors, cooperation and variations among delay factors. These are stakeholder perceptions in a defined project class, not national prevalence estimates for small engineering offices. We use the study to separate causes and to select payment and blocker angles, never to claim that all Iraqi contractors experience the same ranking. [Original study](https://www.joe.uobaghdad.edu.iq/index.php/main/article/view/1847).

A Ministry of Planning field report published **11 June2024** described the new Samawah depot as stalled by liquidity difficulties within the company involved. This is a revealing boundary case: recording a nearly completed project does not resolve the money needed to finish it. We do not present the project’s then-current status as today’s status, generalize from one asset, or suggest Bunyan could have prevented the stoppage. Its editorial use is to make the distinction between a historical transaction record and a forward funding question unavoidable. [Ministry report](https://mop.gov.iq/archives/23508).

## Changes and documents are stronger angles than a generic dashboard tour

The 2019 Erbil public-project study distributed104 questionnaires. Its abstract highlights owner variations in the owner group and unclear drawing detail in the consultant group. “Distributed” is not the same as104 valid responses. The geography, date and public-project population remain attached to the finding. It suggests two practical stories: a change request must expose consequences, and a file must be identifiable to the person using it. It does not prove that naming files reduces delay by a particular amount. [Original Erbil study](https://journals.cihanuniversity.edu.iq/index.php/cuesj/article/view/42).

The 2018 change-order paper uses a30-expert questionnaire and project-document analysis. Its discussion connects changes with scope, time, cost and the timing of decisions. The paper has awkward translation and limited methodological detail. We do not reuse its classifier accuracy, describe all variations as harmful, or recycle the11.3% figure from its literature review as an Iraqi result. The useful mechanism is simpler: “the customer requested it” and “the authorized parties understood and decided its effects” are different records. Our fictional finishing example separates extra delivery days from total project delay because work may overlap. [Original paper](https://opencivilengineeringjournal.com/VOLUME/12/PAGE/458/FULLTEXT/).

## Digital reporting has local precedent, with narrow transferability

An ILO PROSPECTS case study describes engineers using digital monitoring for EIIP sites in Dohuk and Mosul where remote access and security requirements made repeated visits difficult. It is first-party programme reporting, not a controlled study or a Bunyan deployment. The page has no visible publication date; we refuse the extractor’s guessed January2026 date and do not package it as breaking news. The transferable idea is that the information collected must answer the supervising person’s question. More pictures are not automatically a more useful report. [ILO case study](https://prospectslearnings.ilo.org/interventions/employment-intensive-investment-programmes-eiip/case-studies/digital-monitoring-conducted-engineering-students).

The opening Bunyan post consequently asks which of three fictional sites needs the owner’s intervention. One needs a purchasing decision; another lacks a dated, explained update. These require different actions. Our template joins completed work, blocker, decision, named follow-up and review time. Some fields are an agreed writing convention in notes, not dedicated product functionality. We also distinguish remote information from on-site technical inspection. A screen cannot establish concealed workmanship or replace a qualified person’s engineering judgment.

## Structural labour issues are not software features

ILO’s September2025 construction social-security paper draws on the2021 Labour Force Survey and interviews/focus groups. It describes informal work, irregular incomes and barriers to coverage across Federal Iraq and the Kurdistan Region. This is valuable market context but a poor direct Bunyan sales angle: the product’s daily worker count is not payroll, statutory registration, social-security coverage or a verified attendance system. We exclude those claims and do not turn a policy paper into current compliance advice. [ILO policy paper](https://www.ilo.org/publications/extending-social-security-casual-workers-construction-sector-federal-iraq).

ILO’s March2025 labour-based construction management resources include site organization, communication, monitoring and business management. We use that scope to check that our owner content covers handovers and presenting evidence of capability alongside daily execution. The templates are original AURENDOR proposals, not ILO-certified procedures. No external logo should appear as a badge of approval. [Training resource overview](https://www.ilo.org/publications/enhancing-labour-based-construction-management).

## Product fit and what the campaign should make owners believe

The working target is an owner-led contractor or engineering office coordinating several projects and interacting with engineers and clients. This is a campaign hypothesis consistent with Bunyan’s roles and pages, not a newly measured Iraqi segment size. Its immediate product fit is project/stage context, daily reports, expenses and deposits, requests for payment, requests for changes, contextual notes, files and media, client stage interaction and portfolio records. Source evidence is `content-system-v7/exports/P08-complete/FEATURE-COVERAGE.md` and the canonical app’s `src` directory, re-read for the claims used here.

By the end of September, the intended perception is: “These people understand which information affects my decisions, and their product gives my team somewhere to maintain it.” That is more defensible than “this app eliminates delay.” It also leaves room for poor adoption: an empty report or an unrecorded decision remains missing even inside a polished system. B10 therefore provides a seven-day, one-project evaluation with predefined questions, completeness checks, retrieval time and update effort. It explicitly avoids testing memorized questions or advertising unmeasured time savings.

## What still requires local validation

Before presenting any teaching scenario as observed customer research, obtain permission-based interviews with owners, office finance staff and site engineers. Ask for the last disputed change, last returned payment request and last handover—not opinions about whether software is useful. Compare private residential, commercial and public-contract work rather than blending them. Establish who actually enters data, what goes to the client, how frequently updates happen, which decisions need technical or contractual approval, and what connectivity or device restrictions exist. None of those local frequencies has been measured here.

The drafts can be used now as declared educational examples. Seven evidence records passed the source-hash and exact-passage verifier; that checks traceability, not causal truth. The editorial self-review marks experience as requiring further field reporting before a case-study claim, while retaining the explicitly fictional version. Public captions carry a relevant source link; publication dates and product behavior require the preflight specified in each brief. Source rights permit citation and paraphrase, not automatic reuse of article figures, photographs or institutional marks.
